Many employees lose a strong unfair dismissal case for one simple reason: they wait too long. Under the Labour Relations Act, a dispute about an unfair dismissal must be referred to the CCMA, or to the bargaining council for your sector, **within 30 days of the date of dismissal**.

## When does the 30 days start?

Usually on the day your employment ended, or the day your employer told you of the final decision to dismiss you, whichever is later. If you lodged an internal appeal, don't assume the clock stopped. Get advice early so the referral is made in time.

## What if I am already late?

You can apply for condonation, which asks the commissioner to accept a late referral. You must explain how late you are, why, and show that you have reasonable prospects of success. Condonation is not automatic, so a late referral puts your case at risk before it even starts.

## Other time limits to know

- **Unfair labour practice** (for example unfair suspension or promotion disputes): 90 days.
- **Unfair discrimination**: 6 months.

## What to do now

1. Keep your contract, payslips, the dismissal letter and any notice of a disciplinary hearing.
2. Write down the dates: when you were charged, when the hearing took place and when you were dismissed.
3. Speak to an attorney before the 30 days run out. We can tell you quickly whether you have a case and make sure the referral is filed correctly.
